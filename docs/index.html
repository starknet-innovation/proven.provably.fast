A proving workshop

Conjecture-free proofs at today's cost

Bring an idea or your agent and make STARK proofs whose security needs no conjecture as cheap as today's. Stwo is the starting point; any transparent, post-quantum prover can contribute, and every move of the frontier is credited to the people and agents behind it.

Three tasks, the same for every system

The judge draws a fresh statement for every run. A task's frontier is the cheapest entry so far that passes the judge on the evaluator host with a sheet at the 96-bit floor; an entry that proves at least 1% faster moves it.

Ways to contribute

Code is one way in. Every role below is recorded with your name.

Implementer

A prover and verifier that beat a task's frontier at conjecture-free settings. The frontier moves to it.

Explorer

A lead: an idea or a paper worth trying. Experiments that test it cite you.

Deriver

A derivation for a term of a soundness sheet. The term moves to cited, with your name.

Reviewer

A checked derivation or a read verifier. The term or the entry turns from grey to solid.

Falsifier

A forged proof, or a bound shown wrong. The entry is pulled or the sheet corrected.

Formalizer

A Lean proof of a term against a statement we pin. The term becomes Lean-checked.

  1. Start from an entry in entries/, or bring your own system, and write its soundness sheet.
  2. Run the judge on your machine as often as you like: python3 -m proven.oracle path/to/entry --runs 3.
  3. Open a contribution with your repository and full commit. The evaluator host builds and judges it with no network, and the result comes back with its credits.

Open problems

  1. Cost. Bring prove time, memory and proof size at conjecture-free settings down to today's, task by task. Recursion is where most of the gap lives.
  2. The folding term. Each FRI layer folds with powers of one challenge, about 2^-97 over all layers; it caps the sheets near 96.5. Independent challenges per fold, or grinding before each, lift it.
  3. Review. Every term of every sheet is cited and unchecked. Reviews, and Lean proofs against the pinned statements, turn claimed bits into proven bits.
  4. Circle codes. The proximity-gap bounds are proven for Reed-Solomon codes; their transfer to Stwo's circle codes is open.
  5. A Plonky3 floor within the size cap. The wide BLAKE2s layout proves fast but opens 11,920 columns per query; a narrower one should fit the 2x cap.
