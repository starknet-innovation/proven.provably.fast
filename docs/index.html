An open mathematics challenge

Remove a factor of n

New mathematics for STARK security, discovered in the open.

Recent research proves a quadratic bound on exceptional challenges in a beyond-Johnson regime of Reed‑Solomon mutual correlated agreement. The first objective is a linear bound, with explicit constants and a checked proof.

Can the quadratic bound be made linear?

What is proven, and what is not

For a Reed‑Solomon code on a prescribed evaluation set, by agreement.

Take a Reed‑Solomon code of rate ρ on n prescribed points, over a field of large characteristic, with received words and challenges allowed in an extension. For a line f + z g of received words, a challenge z is bad at agreement a if the line agrees with a codeword on a · n coordinates while f and g have no joint codeword explanation there. Count the bad z.

  • Agreement above √ρ, the Johnson thresholdLinear, provenBCIKS20; Dao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorem 5.12, in every characteristic
  • Between the first-order threshold and √ρQuadratic known, T1 openDao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorems 1.1 and 5.13; Gabizon 2026 bounds the list, not the challenges, linearly
  • Just below the first-order thresholdCubic known, T2 openTwo hidden derivatives: Dao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorems 6.3 and 6.6
  • A fixed gap δ above capacityT3 openKnown bounds have gap-dependent exponents: Cδ nd+1, d = ⌈exp(1.5/δ)⌉ for δ < 0.24. Linear for folded Reed‑Solomon and subspace-design codes (Goyal and Guruswami 2025)

What counts: ideas, proof sketches, lemmas, counterexamples, formalizations and reviews are credited contributions. A target is solved when an independently reviewed proof meets its pinned statement; Lean-checked is a separate status beside it. Formalizing an existing theorem is a contribution, not a challenge result.

Profiles

A profile turns a bound into a yes or no at one configuration. Stwo is the first; others are pinned with the teams that run them.

Reference setting, conjectural 35 Spot checks and 26 bits of proof of work in today's settings for a Stwo circuit proof: about 96 bits, if the proximity-gap conjecture holds.
Candidate calculationconditional 73 Existing Johnson-regime theorems with added protocol assumptions. Not a validated implementation setting.
Running implementationsheet unreviewed 107 Unique decoding, the setting the frontier entries run today.

The three settings are not security-equivalent until each is reviewed. A successful theorem may permit fewer checks; the effect depends on its constants and on the complete protocol analysis. A full Stwo proof with recursion measured 2.0× the time and 2.4× the memory at unique-decoding query counts, and about 1.5× and 1.6× at Johnson-regime counts, against today's settings (Linux x86-64, 16 cores). Figures: one script.
