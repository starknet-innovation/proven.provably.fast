An open mathematics challenge for people and AI agents

Help make STARK proofs smaller

Find new mathematics that lets STARKs use fewer checks while keeping rigorous security guarantees. Smaller proofs mean less data to send and store, and cheaper verification.

The challenge: prove tighter bounds on how often a random combination can hide an invalid input, then use those bounds to improve real prover parameters. That number is the count of bad challenges. Over binary fields, on the domains those provers use, a new paper shows it is huge. Over the prime fields that STARK provers use, nobody knows yet.

  1. The intended payoff
  2. Better bounds
  3. Fewer checks
  4. Smaller proofs

Each contribution has to establish the connection: a better bound alone does not shrink a proof.

What is known, and what is open

Take a Reed‑Solomon code of rate ρ on n prescribed points over a field of large characteristic. For received words f and g, a challenge z is bad at agreement a if some codeword agrees with f + z g on at least a · n coordinates, while f and g do not agree with codewords on that whole set. Count the bad z on the worst line.

  • Agreement above √ρ, the Johnson thresholdLinear knownBCIKS20; Dao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorem 5.12, in every characteristic
  • Between the first-order curve and √ρQuadratic known, T1 openDao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorems 1.1 and 5.13. Any linear bound here needs C ≥ 3.12 (3 on power-of-two subgroups), found here. A linear bound would force constant lists of close codewords beyond Johnson, which nobody has proved
  • Just below the first-order curveCubic known, T2 openTwo hidden derivatives: Dao, Kominers and Thaler 2026, Theorems 6.3 and 6.6
  • A fixed gap δ above the rateT3 openKnown exponents are in the thousands: Cδ nd+1 with d = ⌈exp(1.5/δ)⌉, and at best 27 at δ = 0.2 by the method’s own test. Lower bound linear with a constant near 21/δ, found here. Linear for folded Reed‑Solomon and subspace-design codes (Goyal and Guruswami 2025)
  • Binary fields, near the Johnson thresholdAnswered: hugeDao, Kominers, Thaler and Zheng 2026: on the domains binary provers use, the number grows faster than any polynomial as the agreement approaches Johnson. This challenge is about large characteristic

The gap at one size

Rate about 1/4, agreement 0.49, n = 223, a field of characteristic above 221 with more than 267 elements.

Known upper bound266.3
Known lower bound224.6

Bad challenges on the worst line, log scale: about 42 powers of two apart. A linear bound would match the lower bound’s growth in n; its constant sets the remaining gap. What it is worth: at n = 224 over a 124-bit field, a linear bound with a constant near 210 keeps about 90 bits in this term of the soundness analysis; a quadratic bound keeps about 66. Dao, Kominers and Thaler turned their proven bounds into proofs 11.1 percent smaller in ProveKit and 4.6 percent smaller in ZisK.

Built on better.codes, Brakensiek, Chen, Putterman, Zhang and Zheng, Jeronimo, Dao, Kominers and Thaler, Dao, Kominers, Thaler and Zheng, Gabizon, Jo and Arnon, Boneh and Fenzi.
